Designing for a car you don't exactly know

Point a PID controller at "hold 30 meters behind the car ahead" and the design itself is easy. The hard part is that the controller is tuned against a model of the car — a mass, a drag coefficient — and the real vehicle is never exactly that model. Two passengers, a headwind, a half-empty tank: the true mass and drag live somewhere in a range, not at a point.

The controller is designed the same way you'd place poles on a hand-drawn root locus: pick a desired closed-loop damping ζ and natural frequency ωn, plus a third real pole at −p, then solve directly for the PID gains that put the poles there:

k_p = m·(2ζωnp + ωn²) k_i = m·ωn²·p k_d = −c + m·(p + 2ζωn) plant: m·ẍ + c·ẋ = u (mass–drag longitudinal dynamics) loop: L(s) = P(s)K(s), S(s) = 1 / (1 + L(s))

That design is exact for the nominal mass and drag. The question is what happens to it across the whole uncertainty range — and the honest way to answer that isn't to check one or two extreme cases, it's to sample the range widely and track the worst closed-loop behavior found. The sliders above control how wide that range is; every time they move, a fresh batch of sampled vehicles is drawn and the sensitivity of each is solved exactly, in closed form, from its own mass and drag.

The peak of |S(jω)| across frequency is the reciprocal of the stability margin — a controller with a sensitivity peak of 2 can tolerate roughly half as much modeling error as one that peaks at 1. Watching that peak rise as the uncertainty sliders widen is watching the robustness margin shrink in real time.

The lead-vehicle speed trace driving the road above isn't a stand-in — it's the real recorded data: 250 samples at roughly 10 Hz from an actual ACC test drive near Palo Alto, California, pulled directly from the original reftrajdata.mat. One simplification remains, disclosed rather than hidden: the actuator's own dynamic uncertainty, a full frequency-dependent operator in the original design, is reduced here to a random constant gain on the control command.

What isn't simplified is the search itself. This page does not compute the structured singular value μ exactly — that's a real optimization problem, and faking it would be worse than leaving it out. Instead it does exactly what the original analysis's own Monte Carlo section does: draw many plants from the uncertainty set and track the worst one found. More samples tighten the estimate; they never substitute for the exact bound, and the page doesn't claim otherwise.
